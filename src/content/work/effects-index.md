Over two years I made hundreds of these small animations — a number ticking up to a million, a bar chart filling as you watch, a loading spinner, text that flies in and settles. Then I couldn't find them. They'd scattered across folders, named things I no longer remembered.

More than once I sat down, built one from scratch, and realized weeks later I'd already made the same thing months before. That's a specific, stupid, sinking feeling — hours spent on work I didn't need to do, because my own past work was lost to me.

So I did what a library does with books it can't afford to lose: I made an index. Ten short films, each collecting the animations that give you the same feeling — the ones that arrive, the ones that count and pulse, the ones that look like a screen full of live data. Sorted by feeling instead of by date, I could finally picture where something lived before I went looking for it.

Filling ten films properly needed far more animations than I had, and there was no clever way around it. I made two whole versions that didn't work before the third one did — that story is its own post: [I built this three times before it worked](/blog/three-iterations-each-thrown-away/).

One thing you'll notice watching them: they're calm. One shade of blue does all the pointing, everything else stays quiet, and nothing moves without a reason. When the frame holds still, the single thing that's animating is the only thing you look at. The same calm, slowed right down, became its quiet twin: [The Spiritual Series](#spiritual-series).
