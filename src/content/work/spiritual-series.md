This is the same kind of work as [the Effects Index](#effects-index) — small animations made with code — but pointed somewhere completely different, and slowed almost to stillness. Instead of counters and charts, it draws old contemplative patterns: a mandala opening from its center, a knot that never ends, a circle painted in one unbroken stroke. Ten short films, one pattern each, from traditions around the world.

I built it on purpose with the exact same setup as the fast, busy Index — same tools, aimed the opposite way. It's easy to make a method that only fits one kind of subject, so I wanted to see mine tested against its opposite.

The one real change is time. Where the Index moves fast, here everything breathes. Each pattern plays once at its own pace, then plays again slower — because that's how something like this actually goes in. Nobody takes in a mandala faster by being shown it faster. You sit with it, and the second pass is where it lands. Even at that pace every line is still being drawn, still drifting. Quiet, not empty.

I held to a few rules the whole way, because this material deserves care. Everything is drawn as shape, line, and light only — no depictions of any god or figure, in any tradition. Each one gets the exact same treatment and the same amount of room, so none is placed above another. And several of them end by letting go — the sand pattern scatters in the wind, the seal dissolves back to blank paper. That turned out to be the most honest way to close something about the sacred: not with a flourish, but with release.

Why running one way of working at two completely different speeds taught me something is the whole of [I only trusted my method once it survived the opposite](/blog/one-system-two-tempos/).
