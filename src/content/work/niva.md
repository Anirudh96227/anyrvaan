### The problem is a scatter

Your health is not missing. It is everywhere.

Sleep lives in one app. Steps are counted in another. The blood test is filed at a clinic you visited once. Your doctor sees a slice, you see a different slice, and nobody holds the whole picture.

NIVA is an imaginary brand built around fixing that. I wanted the film to make the problem felt before it is explained.

So the film has only two movements.

**Outward** is the problem.
**Inward** is the answer.

### One take, no cuts

The rule I set early was that the camera never cuts.

Every change had to be a morph, a match move or a dive — one thing becoming the next. Health data is disconnected; the film about connecting it couldn't be.

It opens on a single luminous cell — you — splitting apart. The pieces fly outward and slam into glass bell jars, and with every slam a little colour drains out of them. The camera pulls back over a cold field of jars, with one empty jar at the centre where you used to be.

Then it dives into a single jar and lives one day inside it.

> Sleep. Steps. Blood test.

Each one is a small, separate world: a crescent cradle that rocks and wakes, eight footprints walking across the dark, a single red drop falling into still water.

The drop's ripple is the turn. It becomes a heartbeat. The heartbeat travels out across the field, the jars open like petals, warmth floods back in, and every scattered piece streams home.

### Most of it was removing things

Earlier versions had too many ideas competing at once — towers, tunnels, rings, networks, more metaphors than the film could hold.

Almost every pass after that was subtraction.

The copy went from several lines down to one sentence spread across the whole take. The moon in the sleep scene looked cheap, so I explored ten alternatives and kept the one that felt like care rather than decoration: a cradle. "Labs" became "Blood test", because "Labs" meant nothing to anyone outside a hospital. The cold field went through ten versions of its own before the bell jars — trapped, visible, still glowing — said *scattered* more clearly than anything else.

The mandala at the end shrank to four petals: Sleep, HbA1c, Next visit, Steps. Each card slides out of its own petal tip, so the numbers arrive from the exact moments you watched them being made.

### Where the product appears

I didn't want the product to show up as a pasted-on screen at the end.

The four cards grow into the NIVA dashboard itself — a clean white window against the dark film, so it reads as a different, calmer state. You, the orb, keep orbiting it. A cursor taps Sleep, then HbA1c, then *Share with care team*, and the same heartbeat that gathered you now reaches your doctor and the five people around you.

Then everything folds into seven connected cells. That is the mark.

### What I took from it

A product film usually explains features.

This one tries to give the feeling of the problem first — the quiet coldness of being kept in pieces — so that when everything comes home, the relief is the pitch.

Every frame of the film is made with code: no stock footage, no camera, no cuts. But the real work was continuity. One camera, one sentence, one heartbeat, carried from the first frame to the last.
