This is the mirror image of [the field guide to app animations](/work/ui-animation-atlas/). That one collects the moments where an app moves to catch your eye; this one collects a hundred ways an app can do the opposite — stay quiet, wait in the background, and handle things without making you stop and deal with them.

It starts from something most of us feel too often: apps nag. Red dots, badges, pop-ups, buzzes — a constant low tug on your attention, most of it about nothing that matters. So I gathered the alternative: the ways a screen can tell you what you need to know without demanding you look, then get out of the way. There's real relief in a tool that just quietly works, and I wanted to catalogue how that relief gets built.

It's not a science with a textbook — just a real line of thinking I've gathered and turned into things you can actually build, and I was careful to lean only on the parts that hold up. The full argument, and the quiet stretches in my own work that come out of it, is [the best screen is one you barely notice](/blog/the-best-interface-is-no-interface/).
